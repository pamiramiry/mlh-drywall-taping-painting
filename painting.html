Every interior painting job starts with prep: patching, sanding, and priming, before a drop of finish coat goes on. It's the difference between paint that looks good on day one and paint that still looks good a year later, and it's why our finished kitchens and living spaces show clean cutlines around pot lights, cabinetry, and backsplash edges.

Exterior painting follows the same prep-first approach: surface cleaning, scraping and priming bare or worn areas, and coatings chosen for Ontario's weather. Jobs are scheduled around the forecast so the finish cures properly, with careful trim, fascia, and soffit detail work.

Because we also handle drywall repair in-house, colour-matching and touch-up work after a patch is a seamless add-on, with no separate contractor and no mismatched sheen.

What’s included

  • Surface preparationWashing, scuff-sanding, and de-glossing so the new coating has something to grip.
  • Filling and caulkingNail holes, dents, and open trim joints filled and caulked before any paint goes on.
  • PrimingSpot-priming or full priming over patches, bare board, and stains that would otherwise bleed through.
  • Interior workWalls, ceilings, trim, doors, and closets, cut in clean at every edge.
  • Exterior workExterior surfaces prepped and coated with products rated for Ontario freeze-thaw cycles.
  • Protection and cleanupFloors and furniture covered, hardware removed or masked, and the space put back at the end.

How we do it

  1. Protect and prepareFurniture moved or covered, floors protected, hardware and fixtures masked. Most of the visible quality in a paint job is decided before the first coat.
  2. Fill, sand, and caulkHoles and dents filled, patches sanded flush, trim gaps caulked. Every one of these shows through a finish coat if it's skipped.
  3. Prime where neededPatches, bare drywall, and stains get primed so the topcoat lays down at a consistent sheen instead of flashing over the repairs.
  4. Two finish coatsTwo coats of the chosen product, cut in by hand at ceilings, trim, and corners, with a walkthrough before we pack up.

Paint Sheen

Which Paint Sheen Belongs in Which Room

Sheen is the single decision that most affects how a finished room looks, and the one homeowners are least often walked through. Higher sheen wipes clean more easily but shows every imperfection in the wall underneath.

Flat and matte
Hides surface imperfection better than anything else, which makes it the usual choice for ceilings and older walls. Harder to scrub, so a poor fit for a busy hallway.
Eggshell
The common default for living rooms and bedrooms. Slight softness to the light, wipes down reasonably, still forgiving on the wall surface.
Satin
More durable and more washable. Good for hallways, kids rooms and family bathrooms. It starts to reveal wall flaws, so the taping underneath has to be right.
Semi-gloss and gloss
Trim, doors, cabinets and high-moisture rooms. Very washable and very unforgiving: it shows every ridge and fastener, which is why these walls want a Level 5 finish underneath.

The rule that follows: the higher the sheen you want, the better the drywall finishing beneath it has to be. Because we do both the taping and the paint, we can tell you up front whether the wall is ready for the sheen you have picked.
