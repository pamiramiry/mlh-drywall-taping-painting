New builds, additions, and full-home renovations all start the same way: drywall hung plumb, square, and tight to the stud, with the correct board thickness and fire rating for every room. Our crews handle single-room additions up to full-home installs across Scarborough, Toronto, and the GTA, and every board we hang is prepped specifically for the taping and mudding stage that follows: screw pattern, joint spacing, and butt-joint placement all matter to how invisible the final seams end up.

We stock and install standard 1/2" board, moisture-resistant board for bathrooms and basements, and 5/8" fire-rated board where code requires it, like shared walls, garages, and mechanical rooms, so you get one contractor for the whole install instead of coordinating multiple trades.

Every drywall installation quote is free and itemized before we start, and because we also handle the taping, mudding, and painting in-house, there's no handoff between crews and no mismatched standards between the framing-adjacent work and the finished wall.

What’s included

  • Board selectionThe right thickness and type per room, including moisture-resistant board for bathrooms, laundry rooms, and basements.
  • Fire-rated assembliesType X board where garage ceilings, furnace rooms, and shared walls require a rated assembly.
  • Ceiling and wall hangingBoard hung tight to the stud with joints staggered, ceilings supported properly at the wall edge.
  • Correct screw patternFasteners set at the right spacing and driven to depth, dimpled without breaking the paper face.
  • Clean cutoutsTight cuts around outlets, switches, pot lights, vents, and registers so cover plates sit flat.
  • Site cleanupOffcuts and debris cleared as we go, and the room handed to the taping stage ready to work in.

How we do it

  1. Plan the sheet layoutSheets are laid out to keep the number of joints down and to move butt joints off the main sight lines in the room where the layout allows.
  2. Ceilings firstCeiling board goes up before wall board, so the wall sheets tuck under and support the ceiling edge instead of leaving it to the fasteners alone.
  3. Hang and fastenBoard is set tight to the framing and screwed off to the correct pattern, with joints staggered so no seam runs the full height or length.
  4. Check before tapingEvery sheet is checked for gaps, proud screws, and loose board. Fixing it now costs minutes; fixing it after the tape coat costs a day.

Board Types

Choosing the Right Drywall for the Room

Drywall is not one product. Using the wrong board in the wrong room is a common reason a wall fails early, and it is invisible once everything is painted.

Regular board
Standard gypsum board for typical walls and ceilings in living space. Usually 1/2 inch on walls, and 5/8 inch on ceilings to resist sagging over time.
Moisture-resistant
Treated core and facing for bathrooms, laundry rooms and basements. It resists humidity; it is not a tile backer and does not belong inside a shower.
Cement or foam tile backer
What actually goes behind tile in a wet area. Regular or even moisture-resistant board behind a shower will eventually fail.
Type X fire-rated
Required by code in specific assemblies: most commonly garage ceilings and walls shared with living space, furnace rooms, and between units. A code requirement, not an upgrade.
Abuse and impact-resistant
Denser core and tougher facing for hallways, stairwells, garages and commercial corridors that take knocks.
Acoustic board
Damped multi-layer board for bedrooms, home offices, media rooms and party walls, where sound transfer is the problem being solved.

Board thickness, orientation and fastener spacing all follow from the framing behind it. We check what is there before ordering, because the cheapest board is the one that does not have to come back off.
