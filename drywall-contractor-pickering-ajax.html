Pickering and Ajax sit about twenty minutes east of our shop on Scarborough Golf Club Rd, and that proximity is the reason we cover them properly rather than just listing them. It means a real quote visit instead of a number over the phone, and it means coming back for a touch-up is a short trip rather than something we quietly hope you do not ask for.

The housing splits cleanly in two, and the two halves need different work. Bay Ridges, Amberlea and the older parts of Ajax are largely 1970s and 1980s: stipple ceilings, smaller and more broken-up rooms, and basements that were never intended to be lived in. Duffin Heights, Audley and the newer subdivisions are the opposite, with higher ceilings, longer walls, and the finish standard that comes with them.

Basement finishing is the single most common project we take on across both halves, and it is where the difference between them shows up most clearly.

Local Housing

Two Kinds of House, Two Different Jobs

Which half of Pickering or Ajax you are in changes what the job actually is, and it is worth knowing which conversation you are about to have.

1970s and 1980s stock
Stipple ceilings almost as standard, so popcorn removal comes up constantly. Rooms are smaller and more broken up, which genuinely makes the taping easier: short seams with corners to interrupt them are far more forgiving than long ones.
Newer subdivisions
Higher ceilings and open layouts, which brings the same critical-lighting problem you get anywhere with pot lights and long unbroken walls. Level 5 comes up in the conversation far more often here.
Basements and humidity
Being near the lake does not automatically mean a wet basement, but it does mean humidity is worth taking seriously. That shapes the insulation approach and the flooring choice more than it shapes the drywall itself.
Flooring below grade
Vinyl plank rather than laminate, most of the time. Vinyl is synthetic through the whole plank so it tolerates humidity, while laminate has a wood-based core that swells. There is more on this on the flooring page.
Getting here
A straight run on the 401, about twenty minutes. Worth saying plainly, because a contractor who finds your address inconvenient is a contractor who will not come back for the small fixes.

Either way the quote is free and we come out to look at the space rather than pricing it blind.

How a Basement Job Runs Here

  1. Quote on siteWe come out and look. Twenty minutes each way is not a reason to guess at a price.
  2. Check for moisture firstBefore any framing goes up. Finishing over a damp wall hides a problem instead of solving it, and it always comes back.
  3. Frame, insulate, boardWalls held off the concrete and insulated for below-grade conditions, then boarded.
  4. Tape, sand, checkMulti-coat with drying time between coats, then sanded and checked under a raking light.
  5. Floor, trim and paintVinyl plank flooring over a checked subfloor, then trim and two coats.
