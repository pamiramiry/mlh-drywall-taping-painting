Drywall Repair & Patching in Scarborough

Water damage, cracks, holes, and nail pops are patched, re-taped, and texture-matched so the repair blends invisibly into the surrounding wall.

Drywall repair covers everything from a single nail pop to a full section of water-damaged ceiling. We cut out compromised board, replace it, and re-tape and mud the affected area so it disappears into the surrounding wall. Same-visit assessment and a free, itemized quote come before any work starts.

The same multi-coat taping and mudding standard we use on new installs is what makes our repairs disappear into the surrounding wall, with no visible patch outline and no mismatched texture. We match smooth, orange peel, and heavier textures as part of every repair.

If you suspect an active leak behind a water-damaged section, we recommend addressing the source first so the new drywall isn't damaged again. We're happy to advise on that before booking the repair itself.

What’s included

  • Water damageCompromised board cut out and replaced, then re-taped and finished to match the surrounding wall.
  • Cracks and nail popsStress cracks and popped fasteners re-fixed and re-finished rather than just filled over.
  • Holes and impact damageDoorknob holes, furniture damage, and larger openings patched with backed, properly bonded board.
  • Texture matchingExisting finishes matched, whether the wall is smooth, orange peel, or a heavier texture.
  • Ceiling repairsCeiling patches, including around pot lights, vents, and old fixture locations.
  • Paint-ready finishPatches primed and left ready for paint, or repainted as part of the same job.

How we do it

  1. Find the cause firstFor water damage especially, the source gets identified before anything is closed up. New board over an active leak just buys you the same repair twice.
  2. Cut back to sound boardThe damaged area is cut back to solid, dry material on a clean square, with backing added behind the opening where there's no stud to land on.
  3. Patch and re-tapeThe patch is fitted, fastened, and taped to the same multi-coat standard as new work, so the repair isn't the weak spot in the wall.
  4. Match and blendTexture is matched to the existing finish and feathered out past the patch, so the repair reads as wall rather than as a rectangle.

Diagnosing It

What Your Drywall Damage Is Actually Telling You

Patching a symptom without reading the cause is why the same crack keeps coming back. A few patterns are worth recognising before anyone starts filling.

Nail pops
Round bumps or small circular cracks in a line. Usually framing drying and shrinking, especially in a newer build. The fastener has to be re-set properly, not filled over.
Straight cracks along a seam
Movement at a joint, often seasonal. Re-taping the joint is the fix; filler alone will crack again in the same line.
Cracks radiating from a corner
Typically above a door or window opening, and usually movement or settlement rather than a drywall fault. Worth understanding the cause before finishing over it.
Brown or yellow staining
Water, current or historic. The source has to be dealt with first, and the affected board usually replaced rather than sealed over, because gypsum that has been wet does not recover its strength.
Soft or crumbling board
Sustained moisture. This is a replacement, not a patch, and it is worth checking how far the damage travels behind the visible area.
Bubbling or lifting tape
The tape was never properly bedded in compound. It needs to come out and be redone; more mud on top will not bond it.

If you are not sure which of these you are looking at, a photo sent over WhatsApp is usually enough for us to tell you whether it is a patch, a re-tape, or something worth investigating first.
